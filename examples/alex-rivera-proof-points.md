# Proof Points (fictional example)

## Orchard Systems, Director of Support Operations, 2021 to 2024

Scope: team of 14, budget of 2.2 million, reported to the VP of Customer Experience

### Cut first response time
- Number: median first response time from 9 hours to 3 hours between January 2022 and December 2022
- Source: the helpdesk's monthly response time report
- Measures: median time to the first human reply on email tickets. Chat and phone are excluded.
- Limits: ticket volume dropped 12 percent over the same period after a product fix, which helped. I can separate the staffing effect from the volume effect.
- Safe phrasing: "I cut median first response time on email from 9 hours to 3 in 2022. Part of that was lower volume, and I can show you the split."

### Raised customer satisfaction
- Number: CSAT from 84 to 91 percent over 2022 and 2023
- Source: the quarterly survey export
- Measures: share of rated tickets scored 4 or 5 out of 5
- Limits: only about 18 percent of tickets were rated, and the survey wording changed in mid-2022
- Safe phrasing: "CSAT went from 84 to 91 percent on rated tickets. Response rate was low and the survey changed once, and I would walk you through both."

## Larkfield Software, Support Manager, 2018 to 2021

Scope: team of 6, no budget ownership, reported to the head of support

### Built the escalation process
- Number: escalations that missed their target window fell from 31 to 9 percent in the first year
- Source: the escalation tracker I kept in the team's shared spreadsheet
- Measures: share of escalations resolved inside the agreed window
- Limits: the data was tracked by hand, and the window definition changed once during the year
- Safe phrasing: "Missed escalation windows fell from 31 to 9 percent. It was hand-tracked, so I would treat it as directional and tell you how we kept it honest."

## Claims I do not make

- "Cut support costs by 40 percent." The saving came from a vendor contract change that finance negotiated. I cannot claim it.
