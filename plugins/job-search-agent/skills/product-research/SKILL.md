# Product research

A heavier, more granular pass than competitive-research. It covers what a company's products are, what they are called, how they are sold and whether the story the company tells about them holds up against what customers say. The user will want it before a product-focused conversation or when deciding whether a company's product has a real story.

## Start here

Find `job-search-config.md` in the working directory or a connected folder and read it for output preferences. If a competitive-research pass already named the company in this conversation, continue with that company. Otherwise ask which company.

## Research

Use live web search. Go past the marketing site. Read release notes, documentation, review sites and analyst commentary to find what the product does versus what the homepage claims.

Cover the full suite, every distinct product or line, not only the flagship.

1. Product suite overview. Each product or line, one line on what it does.
2. Core mechanics. What the technology actually does, in plain language, with the marketing copy stripped off.
3. Packaging and pricing. Tiers or editions, how it is sold (per seat, usage-based, enterprise-only), and a clear flag if pricing is not public.
4. Segment fit per product. Who buys which one. Look for signals on company size, industry and use case in case studies, review-site filters and job postings that mention the tool.
5. Differentiation claims versus reality. What they say makes them different, checked against reviews, analyst notes and competitor claims. Say whether each claim holds up, partly holds up or does not.
6. Roadmap signal. Recent releases and announcements, and where the visible investment is going.
7. Customer proof. Real themes from review sites and forums about what users praise and complain about at the product level, not the vendor's curated testimonials.
8. The product story. If the user had to explain this product to a customer tomorrow, what is the actual narrative? This is both an interview talking point and an honest check on whether the product has a story or only features.
9. Open questions. What only someone inside the company could answer.

## Sourcing

Tag every factual claim as Verified (name the source) or Inference (a read, not a confirmed fact). Differentiation needs the most care. "They claim X" is a fact about their marketing. "X is true" needs its own verification, or an honest "could not confirm".

## Output

A structured brief in chat, about 900 words, one section per header above. Spend the words on differentiation versus reality, customer proof and the marketing story. Keep the suite overview to a line per product. Do not make a file unless the user asks, and use the format in the config if they do.

Write it like an analyst's working notes, not a vendor comparison chart. Use specific product names, specific claims and specific customer language. If the differentiation story does not hold up, say that directly.
